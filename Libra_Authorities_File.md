## I. Apportionment doctrine (Standard §3.1)

1. ✅ **Sheldon v. Metro-Goldwyn Pictures Corp., 309 U.S. 390 (1940).**
   Proposition: infringer's profits may be apportioned between infringing and non-infringing contributions; "not mathematical exactness but only a reasonable approximation" suffices. *Verify pin page for the quotation (believed ~408).* Libra's foundational principle, extended by express analogy to claimant-side allocation.

2. ⚠️ **Frank Music Corp. v. Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer, Inc., 772 F.2d 505 (9th Cir. 1985)** (and later appeal ~1989).
   Proposition: modern application of Sheldon apportionment; courts accept expert-driven approximation of attributable profits. Verify cite and extract the apportionment-method discussion.

3. 🔎 **Post-Sheldon apportionment line generally** — collect 2–3 modern circuit applications (software/media contexts preferred) showing apportionment by percentage with imperfect proxies. Search terms: "apportionment" + "infringer's profits" + Sheldon.

## II. Plan-of-allocation doctrine — Module A's direct hook (Standard §3.2)

4. ⚠️ **Maley v. Del Global Technologies Corp., 186 F. Supp. 2d 358 (S.D.N.Y. 2002).**
   Proposition: settlement allocation plan approved where it has a "reasonable, rational basis," particularly when supported by experienced counsel. A commonly-cited articulation; verify and shepardize.

5. 🔎 **"Reasonable, rational basis" family (S.D.N.Y. and beyond)** — e.g., *In re WorldCom* and *In re Citigroup* securities settlement opinions. Task: select the 2–3 strongest, most-cited articulations, including at least one circuit-level statement.

6. ✅ **Fed. R. Civ. P. 23(e)** — settlement approval standard ("fair, reasonable, and adequate"), 2018 amendments codifying factors incl. 23(e)(2)(D) equitable treatment of class members relative to each other. *That subsection is Libra's statutory home: differentiated allocation by objective criteria is how equitable relative treatment is achieved.*

7. 🔎 **Securities "recognized loss" allocation plans (the adopted analogy, §3.6).**
   Proposition: formula-driven differentiated allocation by objective per-claimant criteria is routinely approved and does not trigger subclassing. Task: find 2–3 approval opinions expressly describing recognized-loss formulas, plus any commentary describing them as standard practice.

8. ✅ **Amchem Products, Inc. v. Windsor, 521 U.S. 591 (1997)** and ✅ **Ortiz v. Fibreboard Corp., 527 U.S. 815 (1999).**
   Proposition: intra-class conflict / adequacy limits — cite *defensively*: Libra's objective-criteria design is what keeps differentiated allocation on the safe side of these cases.

9. ⚠️ **Bartz v. Anthropic (N.D. Cal., settlement 2025–26).**
   Proposition: the live demonstration of the allocation void — ~$1.5B at flat ~$3,000/work across ~500,000 works; formal objections to allocation fairness; fairness hearing May 14, 2026. Source from docket (CourtListener) + Authors Guild settlement pages; pull the actual plan of allocation and objection filings — primary sources for the article's opening.

## III. Per-work differentiation as doctrinal norm (Standard §3.3)

10. ✅ **17 U.S.C. § 504(c)** — statutory damages attach *per work*, in a range ($750–$30,000; to $150,000 willful), with award size calibrated to circumstances. Proposition: per-work differentiation is the statutory norm; flat-rate is the anomaly. Also anchors the base floor (per-se inclusion value).

11. 🔎 **Statutory damages calibration factors caselaw** — collect one or two standard articulations of factors (willfulness, harm, value of work). Possible anchor: *F.W. Woolworth Co. v. Contemporary Arts, Inc.*, 344 U.S. 228 (1952) (deterrence rationale). Verify fit.

## IV. UK anchors (Standard §3.1, §3.4)

12. ⚠️ **Celanese International Corp. v. BP Chemicals Ltd. [1999] RPC 203 (Laddie J).**
    Proposition: apportionment within account of profits (patent context) — profits attributable to the infringement, not the whole product.

13. ⚠️ **OOO Abbott v. Design & Display Ltd. [2016] EWCA Civ 95.**
    Proposition: Court of Appeal guidance on apportionment in account of profits (design right). Verify cite and extract apportionment reasoning.

14. ✅ **CPR Part 35 + Practice Direction 35.**
    Proposition: expert's overriding duty to the court; obligation to state qualifications, methodology, and *the limits of expertise* — Libra's residual/limits reporting is built to this rule.

15. ⚠️ **"The Ikarian Reefer" [1993] 2 Lloyd's Rep 68.**
    Proposition: classic statement of expert duties (independence, stating assumptions, flagging matters outside expertise). Verify cite.

## V. Admissibility design constraints (Standard §3.4)

16. ✅ **Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals, Inc., 509 U.S. 579 (1993).**
    Proposition: testability, error rates, standards controlling operation, general acceptance — the checklist Libra's mandatory sensitivity analysis and validation-status disclosure are engineered against.

17. ✅ **Kumho Tire Co. v. Carmichael, 526 U.S. 137 (1999).**
    Proposition: Daubert gatekeeping extends to technical/specialized (non-scientific) expertise — i.e., to allocation methodologies.

18. ✅ **Fed. R. Evid. 702** (as amended 2023 — proponent must show reliability by preponderance; expert's opinion must reflect reliable application). Verify current text; the 2023 amendment language is directly favorable to a methodology that publishes its parameters.
